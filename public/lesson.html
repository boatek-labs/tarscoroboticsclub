Breadboards & Building Your First Circuit

In this lesson you will learn how a breadboard works, how to read a simple circuit diagram, and how to wire your first real circuit — an LED with a current-limiting resistor.

🎯 Learning Objectives
  • Understand the internal structure of a breadboard
  • Read a basic schematic diagram
  • Calculate the correct resistor for an LED circuit
  • Wire and test a working LED circuit
  • Understand what a short circuit is and how to avoid it

Materials Needed

Half-size or full breadboard
Arduino Uno (as 5V power source) or 9V battery + connector
2× LED (any colour)
2× 220Ω resistor (red-red-brown-gold)
6× male-to-male jumper wires
Multimeter (optional but recommended)

1. How a Breadboard Works

A breadboard is a reusable prototyping tool that lets you connect electronic components without soldering. The holes are connected underneath by metal strips — understanding this layout is critical before wiring anything.

TIP

The two long rails on the sides (marked + and −) are power rails — every hole in each rail is connected horizontally. The centre rows are connected vertically in groups of 5, separated by the middle gap.

The centre gap separates the two halves — this is where ICs (chips) sit, bridging both sides. Each 5-hole row on one side is internally connected, but the row across the gap is not connected to it.

2. Reading the Schematic

Before you build, always read the circuit diagram. The schematic below shows a simple LED circuit:

5V ──┬── [220Ω] ──┬── LED (+) ──(LED)── LED (-) ──┬── GND
Schematic: LED with current-limiting resistor

The triangle with a bar is the LED symbol. Current flows from + (anode) to − (cathode). The flat edge on the LED and the shorter leg are the cathode (negative side).

3. Calculating the Resistor Value

Without a resistor, too much current flows through the LED and it burns out immediately. Use Ohm's Law:

Formula
// R = (Vs - Vf) / If
R = (Vs − Vf) / If
// For a standard red LED:
Vs = 5V (supply voltage)
Vf = 2V (LED forward voltage)
If = 0.02A (20mA — safe LED current)
R = (5 - 2) / 0.02 = 150Ω → use 220Ω (next standard value up)

4. Wiring the Circuit

Follow these steps carefully. Double-check each connection before powering on.

  • Connect the 5V pin on your Arduino to the + power rail of the breadboard using a red jumper wire.
  • Connect GND to the − power rail using a black wire.
  • Insert the LED so the long leg (anode) goes into row 10, column e; and the short leg into row 12, column e.
  • Insert a 220Ω resistor between the + rail and row 10, column a.
  • Use a jumper wire from row 12, column a to the − power rail.
⚠ WARNING

Never connect power directly to the − rail without a load (resistor or component) in between. Always check polarity before powering on. A short circuit can damage your Arduino permanently.

5. Testing with Arduino (Optional)

If you want to blink the LED using your Arduino instead of just powering it directly, upload this sketch:

arduino · led_blink.ino
// TRC Lesson 7 — Blink an LED const int LED_PIN = 9; void setup() { pinMode(LED_PIN, OUTPUT); } void loop() { digitalWrite(LED_PIN, HIGH); // LED on delay(500); // wait 500ms digitalWrite(LED_PIN, LOW); // LED off delay(500); }
✓ CHECK

If your LED doesn't blink, first check: Is the LED in the right direction? Is the resistor connected between power and the LED's anode? Is pin 9 selected in your sketch and matching your physical wiring?
